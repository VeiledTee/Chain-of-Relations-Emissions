# Contribution 2 Analysis Checkpoint

Date: 2026-09-29

This document records the frozen state of Contribution 2 (C2). It is not a new
analysis. Git tag: `c2-analysis-complete-2026-09-29`. Work after this checkpoint
belongs to Contribution 3 (C3) and happens on the `c3-measurement-guided-optimizations`
branch.

## Scope

C2 characterizes and explains GPU energy use in existing agentic KGQA systems
(PoG, ToG, CoR). C2 does NOT modify the systems in order to improve efficiency.

The clean boundary is:

- **C2:** measurement, attribution, characterization, explanation.
- **C3:** intervention, redesign, optimization, and evaluation of modified systems.

## Research question

> "When inference is embedded in a multi-step agentic loop over a knowledge
> graph, where is energy actually spent?"

- **RQ2a:** operation-level, hardware-grounded energy decomposition across
  reasoning, tool use, graph retrieval/search, and trajectory progression.
- **RQ2b:** comparison of KGQA paradigms on the effectiveness–energy frontier.

## Canonical evaluation

One evaluator, `chain_of_relations/eval/accuracy.py`, scores every system on
every dataset (driven per prediction file by `measurement/export_outcomes.py`).
WebQSP-specific gold handling lives in `chain_of_relations/eval/webqsp_canonical.py`.

- **Only actual answers are scored.** For CoR, comma/semicolon-split JSON
  fragments are first rejoined and the JSON `"answer"` field is extracted.
  Plain final-answer outputs (PoG/ToG) are accepted directly.
- **Rationale text is never scored.** Rationale without an `"answer"` field
  receives no answer credit.
- **Matching** is normalized exact equality (lowercase, punctuation removed,
  articles removed, whitespace collapsed), NOT substring matching.
- **Hits@1:** 1 if any extracted predicted answer exactly matches any gold answer
  after normalization.
- **Precision:** distinct correct predictions / distinct predictions.
- **Recall:** distinct gold answers predicted / distinct gold answers.
- **F1:** harmonic mean of precision and recall (empty sets follow the official
  WebQSP `CalculatePRF1` rules).
- **WebQSP:** every official parse; P/R/F1 from the best-F1 parse, Hits@1 over
  the union of parses. **CWQ:** the answer list shipped with the dataset.
- **KGR† = 1 − fallback rate.** An operational metric (share of questions answered
  without the closed-book fallback), not the exact KG-grounded Rate defined in
  the CoR paper.

This evaluator replaced an earlier substring-matching scorer that could credit
gold names appearing in rationale text and counted rationale fragments as
predictions, affecting the three systems by different amounts.

## Measurement scope

- GPU energy only (NVML cumulative counter, one RTX 4090). CPU package, DRAM and
  total operational energy are not measured and are not claimed.
- Question-level energy is reported in Wh/question (whole-question trajectory
  counter); operation-level energy is attributed event energy.
- Model: Gemma-3-4B (`google/gemma-3-4b-it`, revision `093f9f38…`).
- WebQSP: N = 1,639 questions per system, maximum search depth 3.
- CWQ: N = 3,520 questions per system, maximum search depth 4.

## C2 findings

1. Architecture changes both effectiveness and GPU energy use.
2. PoG has the lowest mean GPU energy per question on both datasets.
3. On WebQSP, CoR is strongest across H@1, precision, recall, F1 and operational
   KGR, at a moderate energy increase over PoG (0.386 vs 0.271 Wh/q).
4. On CWQ, PoG and CoR have similar mean energy (0.614 vs 0.632 Wh/q) and close
   F1 (difference not statistically resolved), while CoR has higher H@1, recall
   and operational KGR.
5. ToG consumes substantially more GPU energy on both datasets (0.718 and
   1.310 Wh/q) without leading the main effectiveness metrics.
6. Task-level energy hides architecture-specific internal profiles:
   - ToG: `entity_prune` + `relation_rank` (~46% + ~40–42% of attributed energy);
   - CoR: `relation_rank` + reasoning (~50–51% + ~39–40%);
   - PoG: `relation_rank` dominant (~51–60%), with the remaining energy
     distributed across several stages.
7. Operation energy depends on both cost per execution and number of executions.
8. ToG `entity_prune` is an output-heavy hotspot: roughly a 20–22× median
   per-call energy difference relative to PoG pruning (20.4× WebQSP, 22.0× CWQ),
   closely tracking its output-token difference.
9. `relation_rank` is consistently expensive per call across systems
   (~0.086–0.102 Wh/call). Differences in total ranking energy arise largely
   from invocation frequency; candidate/context workload explains additional
   per-call variation.
10. Longer trajectories cost more mainly because they execute more operations,
    not because late-stage calls become dramatically more expensive.
11. Exact duplicate `relation_rank` calls occur in PoG and ToG and account for
    measurable trajectory energy (PoG 2.7% WebQSP / 6.9% CWQ; ToG 2.1% / 3.2%).
    CoR has no exact duplicate `relation_rank` calls in the measured runs.
12. CoR reasoning accounts for roughly 40% of CoR energy but is NOT currently
    demonstrated to be inefficient or redundant.

## C2 limitations

- GPU-only energy measurement; no CPU/DRAM or total operational energy.
- WebQSP and CWQ have different gold/evaluation structures (multi-parse best-F1
  vs a single answer list); absolute effectiveness is not comparable across them.
- Maximum search depth differs between datasets (3 vs 4).
- KGR is operationalized as 1 − fallback rate, not the paper's literal definition.
- Exact matching is name-level (not Freebase MID) and does not split
  multi-entity answer strings; the CWQ CoR/PoG F1 ordering is sensitive to this.
- Observational hotspot evidence does not prove that removing a computation will
  preserve effectiveness.
- Measured correlations must not be presented as causal intervention results.

## Explicit C2 → C3 boundary

C2 ends with identifying and explaining candidate optimization targets.

C3 begins when an existing system is modified and rerun to test whether energy
can be reduced while preserving effectiveness.

Examples of C3 work — NOT C2 results:

- caching exact duplicate `relation_rank` calls;
- deduplicating search/frontier work;
- shortening or removing the relation-ranking rationale;
- removing unused PoG relevance-score generation;
- reducing candidate/context workload;
- modifying stopping/search policies;
- redesigning reasoning/search stages.

These are hypotheses motivated by C2, not findings of C2.
